Draw the relationship, not the decoration

A reference set for explanatory sections, English edition. The example is a team handbook that several people edit. It shows three relationships the other editions do not: levels across a grid, positions on two axes, and the states a draft can move between.

01Requirement matrix02Two-axis map03State transitions

Use it for a section that explainshow something works, steps and their feedback, structure, or a comparison, when a figure says it faster than a short paragraph.

Don't apply it to a whole pageArticles, forms, search and data browsing keep their own layouts. On a mixed page, only the explanatory section uses it.

Brand guidelines, an existing design system and the reader's or client's stated style come first.

Which checks does a change need?

Rows are kinds of change, columns are kinds of check. Each mark answers one question: is this check needed here?

  • Shape and fill carry the level, so it survives grayscale.
  • On a phone the grid stays a grid; only the notes drop.

Fig. 02

How far does a change travel?

Two independent questions place each change: who will see it, and how hard it is to take back. The corner where both are high asks for the most care.

  • An empty cell says why it is empty.
  • The plane deepens toward care, without numbers.

Fig. 03

Where can a draft go next?

Boxes are states, labels on the lines are what moves a draft between them. A request for changes goes back to the draft, not to the start of a new one.

  • Name the trigger on the line itself.
  • Dead ends look different from the main path.
